# AISB Vegas 2026 e-paper badge

This revision is a 100 x 145 mm conference badge built around the
[Waveshare 3.52-inch raw black/white e-paper panel](https://www.waveshare.com/3.52inch-e-paper.htm).
The screen is large enough for a readable name and role, while the upper PCB
remains visible for the AISB logo and copper artwork.

The badge has no microcontroller, USB connector, battery, MIDI keys, or touch
controls fitted by default. Instead:

- the 360 x 240 e-paper glass is centred on the front of the badge;
- its actual 3.65 +/- 0.30 mm electrical FPC, as dimensioned in the
  [Waveshare V1.1 panel drawing](https://files.waveshare.com/wiki/3.52inch%20e-Paper%20HAT/3.52inch%20e-Paper%20V1.1.pdf), stays straight into a low-profile
  front-side ZIF immediately left of the glass; the drawing's 10 mm feature is
  only a removable adhesive-tape pull-tab, and there is no internal flex
  cutout;
- the badge carries the small passive high-voltage support circuit required by
  the raw panel;
- a reusable external programmer supplies 3.3 V, ground, and SPI/control through
  the zero-height Tag-Connect `J3` target;
- optional `J4` is a passive 1 x 8, 2.54 mm through-hole interface for a short
  ribbon harness to a removable Waveshare ESP32 Driver Board V3; the module is
  powered only through its own USB-C connector, its onboard FPC stays empty,
  and the carrier provides only passive slots and a nonconductive spacer/retainer;
  and
- a two-layer PCB pickup coil remains available as a passive electromagnetic
  audio sensor.

After an external programmer refreshes the screen and is removed, the image
remains visible without power. Each badge therefore needs the comparatively
expensive controller only while its name is being written.

## Important status

This is a prototype design, not a production release. The current 24-pin panel
table and an older application-circuit drawing in Waveshare's documentation
are not completely consistent. Before ordering production quantity:

1. Buy one exact panel SKU/revision and the proposed 24-position ZIF.
2. Verify the flex contact side, pin 1, insertion direction, glass dimensions,
   and complete straight-tail/connector fit against the physical parts.
3. Assemble 3-5 badges and validate every generated e-paper rail during a
   refresh with a current-limited bench supply.
4. Confirm that the display survives repeated mounting and programming cycles.
5. Run KiCad DRC and have an experienced PCB designer review the high-voltage
   support circuit, footprint geometry, and assembly files.
6. If the optional ESP32 path is considered, measure at least three exact Rev 3
   modules, continuity-test the passive harness, and validate the mounting
   slots/spacer, USB clearance, retention/drop loads, and worn-badge RF range.
   There is no onboard power isolation: never attach or power two controllers.

Do not send the generated board straight to a production run.

For the current two-week deadline, follow
[`docs/two-week-release-plan.md`](docs/two-week-release-plan.md). The custom
on-board circuit remains the primary path, with five engineering units tested
first. Every raw panel is also pre-written off-board before mounting. Two
official Waveshare Driver HAT stations provide an independent fallback by
writing the raw panel flex directly; they do not bypass the badge through
`J3`. See [`docs/hat-fallback.md`](docs/hat-fallback.md).

For the two-week schedule, buy **both types of universal driver**: two
[Universal Driver HATs](https://www.waveshare.com/e-paper-driver-hat.htm) as the
Raspberry-Pi-friendly production fallback, and at least three exact
[ESP32 Driver Board V3](https://www.waveshare.com/e-paper-esp32-driver-board.htm)
samples for dimensional spread, passive-harness validation, and
participant-add-on experiments. Waveshare's 3.52-inch panel page recommends
both, but the ESP32
product page's current support table does not explicitly list 3.52 inches, so
do not depend on the optional module until the exact parts pass the complete
physical, electrical, and repeated-refresh gates.

## Open the design in KiCad

1. Install KiCad 8 or newer.
2. In KiCad's main window choose **File > Open Existing Project**.
3. Select `vegas26-badge.kicad_pro` in this directory.
4. Double-click **PCB Editor** to inspect the board. The circuit reference is
   the legacy `vegas26-badge.sch`; open that file from Schematic Editor and
   let KiCad convert it when prompted.
5. In PCB Editor, press **B** once to refill the rear ground zone, then run
   **Inspect > Design Rules Checker**.
6. Press **Alt+3** (or choose **View > 3D Viewer**) for the
   board preview.

Do not run **Update PCB from Schematic**. The board, including distributed
pickup `L1`, is deterministically generated by `scripts/generate-board.mjs`;
the legacy schematic is an electrical reference rather than its netlist source.

The PCB outline, footprints, copper, pads, and silkscreen are in the KiCad
source. Vendor 3D models are not assigned to every custom footprint, so
KiCad may omit the glass, ZIF, or reusable programming cable. The SVG renders
and OpenSCAD model show the intended assembled form.

## Layout

```text
front, 100 x 145 mm

      lanyard hole
  +------------------+
  | passive PCB coil |  <- copper artwork / EM pickup
  |      AISB.       |
  |------------------|
  |  3.52-inch raw   |  <- 360 x 240 persistent name/role
  |  e-paper glass   |
  |                  |
  +------------------+

front: centred panel plus short-flex ZIF beneath the left service spine
back:  passive e-paper support parts, audio pads, a connector-free
       Tag-Connect target, optional DNP 1 x 8 J4, and passive module mounting
```

The panel is centred at `(50.00, 94.205)` mm, so its 84.70 mm landscape width
runs from `x=7.65` to `x=92.35` mm. Front-side `J1` sits immediately left of
the glass and uses the
[Hirose FH34SRJ-24S-0.5SH](https://www.hirose.com/en/product/p/CL0580-1255-6-50)
candidate, rotated 270 degrees so its mouth faces right. Its mouth is
near `x=5.50`, body spans about `x=1.70..5.50`, signal-pad row is near
`x=5.00`, and retention row is near `x=1.70`. The inserted tail tip reaches
`x=4.00`, giving 1.50 mm nominal insertion past the mouth. The short electrical
FPC crosses the small gap without bending. The PCB remains solid beneath the
glass and has no display or flex-tail cutout.

The custom `J1` footprint numbers its pads by the Waveshare panel contacts
(panel pin 1 at the top of the landscape tail). In this right-facing use that
sequence is mirrored relative to the Hirose terminal numbering. Follow the PCB
panel-pin marking, not the connector body's polarity mark, and continuity-check
the first physical assembly before applying power.

The intended no-hole mount looks like a small instrument viewport: a black
PETG spacer/bezel surrounds the glass, a 0.5 mm clear hard-coated polycarbonate
lens protects it, and thin perimeter transfer adhesive holds the stack. An
opaque removable all-plastic left service spine, keyed to the bezel, hides and
protects the front connector while preserving access to `J1`. Do not use
magnets or steel clips; they would disturb the passive magnetic pickup.

## Programming architecture

The rear `J3` pads mate with a reusable TC2050-IDC-NL spring-pin ribbon cable;
nothing is soldered there on each badge. The external programmer must provide
a regulated 3.3 V rail plus MOSI, SCLK, CS, D/C, reset, and the active-low BUSY
input. It must never apply 5 V.

The panel's raw 24-conductor tail is **not** the exposed SPI interface. It also
carries its internal gate/source drive and boosted display rails, so it stays
connected to low-profile front-side ZIF `J1` immediately left of the glass.
See `docs/pinout.md` for the exact separation.

### Optional removable rear ESP32 controller

> **NOT PRODUCTION RELEASED:** `J4`, its harness, and the passive module mount
> remain optional. Qualify the exact delivered module, pin map, USB clearance,
> retention/drop behavior, and worn-badge RF range before participant use.

The physical-rear-right bay accepts the current `29.46 x 48.25 mm` Waveshare
e-Paper ESP32 Driver Board V3 in passive carrier slots with a removable,
nonconductive spacer/retainer. It does not plug electrically into the carrier.
Keep its USB-C connector accessible below the badge edge and its antenna end
clear. The module's onboard 24-pin FPC connector stays empty: the one raw panel
remains connected only to front-side badge `J1`.

Optional badge `J4` is one row of eight 2.54 mm plated holes. Populate a simple
1 x 8 male header only on equipped badges, or solder a short harness directly
to the holes. A short 8-way female-female Dupont ribbon can mate to the badge
header and split into individual female sockets at the Waveshare end. Its badge
pin order is `1=3V3`, `2=GND`, `3=BUSY`, `4=RST`, `5=DC`, `6=CS`, `7=MOSI`,
`8=SCLK`; [`docs/pinout.md`](docs/pinout.md) gives the exact Waveshare `J3/J4`
header mapping. Label pin 1 and continuity-test every harness before use.

This is a deliberately passive interface: there is no onboard current limiter,
power mux, buffer, or controller arbitration. The Waveshare board is powered
only by its own USB-C connector, and its `VDD3V3` output supplies badge `3V3`
through `J4-1`. Remove the Tag-Connect probe and every other supply before
connecting the harness. Connect and disconnect only while fully unpowered, and
never attach or power two controllers at once.

Published Rev 3 hardware leaves GPIO4's `R35` link DNP, and this badge does not
connect GPIO4. Firmware therefore cannot switch off the USB-powered target; a
successful update means the panel entered deep sleep while the module and badge
rail remain powered. Disconnect USB-C before removing the harness. Set the
module's USB-to-UART switch 2 **ON** for its CH343 bridge. Test at least 20
refresh/sleep cycles, BUSY-timeout recovery, harness strain relief, USB cable
side-load, retention, shake/drop, and radio range on a fully stacked worn badge.

## Passive pickup

The upper copper artwork is a 30-turn-per-layer, series-aiding magnetic search
coil. Its large chamfered rectangle uses the badge's available area more
effectively than a circular loop. The theoretical open-circuit pickup is about
1.9x the original 12-turn-per-layer revision. Ground pours are explicitly kept
out of the winding area. Production turns remain under continuous black solder
mask so fingers, sweat, or metal badges cannot bridge adjacent turns; renders
show their path more strongly only so the shape is understandable.

Hold the upper half near a laptop, display cable, charger, motor, or other
low-voltage electronic device, then rotate the badge for the loudest result.
Record through a microphone input or USB microphone adapter. `C1` is 4.7uF to
preserve 50/60Hz content while blocking microphone bias, `R1` provides the
nominal headset-microphone detection load, and `R2` joins the two spiral layers.
Use a short twisted or shielded audio pigtail, and do not refresh the e-paper
while recording.

See `docs/pickup-design.md` for the shape comparison, predicted sensitivity,
CTIA wiring, and prototype acceptance tests.

It is a sensor, not test equipment. Never touch it to exposed conductors or use
it inside mains-powered equipment.
